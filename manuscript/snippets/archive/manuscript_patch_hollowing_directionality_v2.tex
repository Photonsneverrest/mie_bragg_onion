\subsection{Material-efficient hollow Bragg onions and scattering directionality}
Progressive replacement of inner radial domains by air showed that the backward
structural-colour response is governed predominantly by the outer radial reflector.
For the $\lambda_0=470$~nm reference with $N_{\mathrm{shell}}=120$ and
$n_A/n_B=1.55$, replacing 110 nominal inner domains by air preserved a broad blue
backward-scattering band while leaving only five high-index A domains in the outer
reflector. Because material B has $n_B=n_m=1$, only removal of the A domains changes
the optical structure, and hollowing is most naturally reported by the number of
remaining high-index domains rather than by the nominal number of replaced domains.

The air-filled $h=110$ geometry remained forward dominated. The hollow region was
therefore filled with a material of refractive index
$\widetilde n_{\mathrm{fill}}=n_{\mathrm{fill}}+i\kappa_{\mathrm{fill}}$, while the
outer reflector was left unchanged. A sweep over $n_{\mathrm{fill}}=1.00$--1.55 and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fill}}=3\times10^{-4}$--$2\times10^{-2}$ revealed a continuous
trade-off between attenuation of the forward response, retention of the backward
spectrum, and colour change. Ranking all cases using same-wavelength attenuation at
the air-hollow backward peak, blue-band backward retention, visible-spectrum
correlation, and $\Delta E_{76}$ identified weakly absorbing intermediate-index
fills as the most colour-preserving solutions. The leading candidate,
$n_{\mathrm{fill}}=1.20$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fill}}=5\times10^{-4}$, attenuated
the forward response at the reference backward peak by 0.55~dB, retained 98.2\% of
the backward response at that wavelength and 102.0\% of the blue-band backward
integral, and produced $\Delta E_{76}=2.55$. Increasing the extinction coefficient
to $10^{-3}$ increased the attenuation to 0.67~dB while retaining 95.9\% of the
backward response at the reference peak and 98.2\% of the blue-band backward
integral. A liquid-like fill with $n_{\mathrm{fill}}=1.33$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fill}}=10^{-3}$ provided a similar compromise, with 0.69~dB
attenuation at the reference peak, 95.8\% same-wavelength backward retention, and
$\Delta E_{76}=4.81$.

To determine whether absorption can remove forward scattering without breaking
spherical symmetry, the hollow onion was compared with equal-radius homogeneous
spheres using volume-permittivity and Bruggeman effective indices. The $h=110$
geometry has diameter $23.197~\mu$m and high-index volume fraction 0.398; the two
effective-index estimates are 1.062 and 1.045, respectively. For the homogeneous
controls, moderate absorption reduced the maximum total scattering efficiency from
approximately 2.4--2.5 to approximately 1.0--1.2, but did not eliminate the forward
response. The mean asymmetry parameter increased to approximately 0.99 for
$\kappa=0.01$--0.1, showing that the residual scattering became strongly forward
directed. At very large extinction, the asymmetry decreased again while the total
scattering increased, consistent with an opaque-particle diffraction limit rather
than complete suppression of scattering. Thus, isotropic absorption in a spherical
particle can damp resonances and reduce the scattering magnitude, but cannot remove
the geometrical forward-diffraction contribution. Stronger selective suppression
would require angular or spatial symmetry breaking and therefore lies outside the
concentric multilayer-Mie model.

The corrected angular integration satisfies
$Q_{\mathrm{forward}}+Q_{\mathrm{backward}}=Q_{\mathrm{sca}}$ to numerical
precision for all homogeneous controls. The hollow multilayer reference retains a
non-zero lossless residual in the present Scattnlay calculation; absolute values from
that control are therefore used only after profile- or wavelength-level numerical
screening, whereas the homogeneous controls provide the quantitative diffraction
baseline.
